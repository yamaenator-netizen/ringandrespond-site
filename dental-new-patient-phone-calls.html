New patient call FAQs

How many new patients call after hours?

Roughly 45% of all patient calls arrive outside business hours — and new patients disproportionately call in the evening, when they're off work and comparing practices. Those are the highest-intent callers you'll ever get, and they're calling when your office is dark.

Do new patients leave voicemails?

Only about 14% do. The rest hang up and call the next practice on their list. A new patient who reaches voicemail is not a callback for tomorrow — they're someone else's patient by tomorrow.

What does a new patient ask on the first call?

The same five things: Are you taking new patients? Do you take my insurance? Where are you located? When's your next opening? How much will it cost? Sunny answers all five from your approved answers, then captures the request — in one call.

Can an AI book a new patient appointment?

Not directly. Sunny captures new-patient requests live on the call, following your scheduling rules — providers, new-patient blocks, emergency slots. She collects the details your team needs (reason for visit, contact info, preferred time) so the request arrives complete for your team to confirm.

What about high-value calls like implants or Invisalign?

Those are exactly the calls you can't afford to miss — a single implant case can be worth $3,000–$6,000. Sunny treats every new-patient call as high priority: answered on the second ring, captured with full detail, flagged in your morning summary.

Will new patients trust an AI on the first call?

New patients judge the call by one thing: did I get taken care of? Sunny introduces herself as your after-hours assistant, answers their questions, and captures their request and preferred time. Callers who get helped in 90 seconds don't ask about the technology — they're glad someone answered.

How fast should a practice respond to a new patient inquiry?

Immediately. Every minute of delay drops conversion — and voicemail is an infinite delay. Live answer is the only response time that consistently wins the patient.

How do dental offices lose new patients on the phone?

Three ways: the call isn't answered at all, it lands in voicemail (only about 14% of callers leave one), or it's answered rushed by a busy front desk. New patients judge the practice by that first call — a competitor who answers warmly wins the appointment.

What information should be captured on a new patient call?

Name, callback number, what they're calling about, and the appointment window they prefer. That's enough for your team to confirm the booking in the morning. Insurance IDs and dates of birth stay out of the first call — your office collects those through its own process.
