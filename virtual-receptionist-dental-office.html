What a virtual receptionist does for your practice

A virtual receptionist is a remote front desk for your phones: answering every call in your practice's name, booking appointments, answering routine questions (hours, location, insurance), taking detailed messages, and escalating urgent calls. Your in-office team stops splitting attention between the patient in the chair and the phone ringing — because the phone is already handled.

There are two kinds: human virtual receptionists (remote agents working your account in shifts) and AI virtual receptionists (voice AI answering instantly, around the clock). Here's how they compare:

When each makes sense

  • Choose human if most of your calls are emotionally sensitive or complex, and you want a person making judgment calls on every one.
  • Choose AI if your volume is routine scheduling, after-hours coverage, and overflow — the calls where speed and consistency beat conversation.
  • Choose both the way most modern practices do: AI absorbs the volume (nights, weekends, lunch, rushes) and your in-office humans handle in-person care and judgment calls.

The practices getting the most from virtual reception aren't replacing anyone — they're covering the calls that currently go unanswered. Industry data puts missed-call rates at 32–38%; a virtual receptionist turns those into booked appointments.

Virtual receptionist FAQs

What does a virtual receptionist do for a dental office?

A virtual receptionist handles your practice's phone calls remotely — answering on your behalf, booking appointments, taking messages, and routing urgent calls. Human virtual receptionists work scheduled shifts; AI virtual receptionists like Sunny cover 24/7 without shifts, breaks, or sick days.

How much does a virtual receptionist cost for a dental office?

Human virtual receptionist services typically run $300–$1,000+/month depending on minutes and coverage hours. AI receptionists range from about $149 to $1,299/month. Ring & Respond is $0 setup plus $199/month for After-Hours Capture or $299/month for 24/7 Complete, flat — 24/7, no per-minute fees.

Human virtual receptionist vs AI — which is better?

Humans bring judgment and empathy to sensitive calls; AI brings instant pickup, unlimited simultaneous calls, and flat pricing. For routine scheduling and after-hours coverage, AI usually wins on cost and consistency. Many practices use AI for volume and keep humans for in-person care.

Can a virtual receptionist book dental appointments?

Yes. Sunny books live on the call using your scheduling rules — providers, new-patient blocks, emergency slots. Human virtual receptionists can too, though usually through your PMS or a callback workflow rather than instant booking.

Does a virtual receptionist work after hours?

AI virtual receptionists do — 24/7/365 at the flat rate. Human services typically charge premium tiers for evenings, weekends, and holidays, or don't cover them at all.

Do I keep my existing phone number?

Yes. Your office number forwards to the virtual receptionist whenever you choose. Patients never dial anything new.

Will patients know it's a virtual receptionist?

Sunny introduces herself as your after-hours assistant and answers honestly if asked whether she's AI. Most callers just get booked and hang up — the experience feels like a good front desk, not a robot.

How quickly can a virtual receptionist start?

Ring & Respond practices typically go live within days: a 2-minute onboarding captures your hours, services, emergency rules, and scheduling preferences, then you forward your line. Human services often need 1–2 weeks for agent training on your practice.

What's the difference between a virtual receptionist and a remote front-desk employee?

A remote employee is one person working set hours from home — they still get sick, take lunch, and can only take one call at a time. A virtual receptionist service covers the role itself: every call answered, including simultaneous calls, evenings, and weekends, with no hiring or training.

Can a virtual receptionist work with Dentrix, Eaglesoft, or Open Dental?

Sunny captures the appointment request — patient, reason, preferred window — and delivers it to your team, who confirm it in Dentrix, Eaglesoft, Open Dental, or whatever you run. She doesn't write directly into your practice software, and any vendor who promises that on day one deserves a hard follow-up question.

Does a virtual receptionist cover lunch breaks and days off?

Yes — that's one of its main jobs. Lunch hour is when working patients call, and it's exactly when a small front desk is empty. Overflow coverage catches those calls the same way it catches after-hours calls.
