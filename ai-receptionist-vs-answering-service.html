When each option wins

Choose a traditional answering service if…

  • Your callers are often distressed and you want a human voice first, every time.
  • You only need message-taking, not booking — and your morning team reliably works the callbacks within the hour.
  • You've negotiated a genuinely flat rate with no per-minute billing.

Choose an AI receptionist if…

  • You want calls resolved, not relayed — appointments booked overnight, not message slips.
  • Your front desk is drowning in routine calls (hours, insurance, rescheduling) during the day too.
  • You want predictable cost: one flat rate regardless of call volume.
  • You need emergency triage that follows your exact protocol at 2 AM.

Sunny shouldn't give clinical advice, negotiate a billing dispute, or counsel an anxious patient alone — and she won't try. She's configured to recognize those moments and hand off to your team with the full conversation context, so nobody starts from zero. The practices that love AI receptionists keep humans for in-person care and judgment calls, and let the AI absorb everything repetitive.

AI receptionist vs answering service FAQs

What is the difference between an AI receptionist and a dental answering service?

A traditional answering service has human operators who take a message for your team to handle later. An AI receptionist like Sunny conducts the call herself — booking appointments, answering questions, and routing emergencies live, following your rules. The difference is what happens after the call is answered: a message slip vs. a handled call.

Is an AI receptionist better than a human answering service for dentists?

For routine and after-hours volume, usually yes: the AI answers instantly with no queue, books directly instead of taking messages, and costs a flat monthly rate. Human services are better at sensitive or complex conversations. Many practices keep their front desk for in-person care and use AI for everything the desk can't cover.

Can an AI receptionist book dental appointments?

Sunny books routine appointments live on the call using your scheduling rules — providers, emergency slots, new vs. existing patients. Anything she can't book becomes a detailed request for your team, not a vague message slip.

How much does each option cost?

Traditional answering services run $200–$600/month plus per-minute charges. AI receptionists range from ~$149 to $1,299/month. Ring & Respond is $499 one-time setup plus $299/month flat, with a two-week money-back pilot.

Will patients prefer a human operator?

Patients prefer whoever solves their problem fastest. An operator who takes a message means waiting until morning; an AI that books the appointment on the spot usually wins. For emotionally sensitive calls, Sunny hands off to your team with full context.

What can't an AI receptionist do?

Honestly: it shouldn't give clinical advice, negotiate billing disputes, or handle deeply upset patients alone. Sunny is configured to recognize those situations and route them to your team immediately — she knows what she doesn't know.

Do I need to change my phone number to switch?

No — for either option. Your existing office number forwards to the service after hours (or whenever you choose). Patients never dial anything new.

AI answering service vs live answering service — what's the real difference?

A live answering service employs operators who take messages, usually billed per minute, with quality that varies by operator and shift. An AI receptionist follows the same approved flow on every call, answers instantly with no hold queue, and is billed flat — but it works within defined rules rather than improvising like a person.

Which option handles simultaneous calls better?

AI, and it isn't close. A live service has a finite operator pool, so a Monday-morning rush means hold music. Sunny answers unlimited simultaneous calls, so five callers at 9 AM get five answered calls.

Who writes down what the caller actually needed?

With a traditional service, you get whatever the operator typed. With Ring & Respond, every call is captured the same way — name, number, reason, requested window — and summarized for your team, so nothing depends on one operator's note-taking.
