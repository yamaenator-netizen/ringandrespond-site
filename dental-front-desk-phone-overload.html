The math your front desk lives every day

A dental front desk staffer checks in patients, verifies insurance, collects payments, manages the schedule — and answers every call. Industry call-tracking data shows practices miss 32–38% of calls, mostly during business hours, because the person who should answer is with a patient. Nobody's slacking; the job as designed is impossible.

The symptoms are familiar: phones ringing unanswered at 10 AM, patients on hold getting frustrated, staff choosing between the person at the window and the caller — and losing either way. Then the burnout, then the turnover, then training someone new into the same impossible job.

What actually eats their day

  • Scheduling and rescheduling — the single biggest chunk, and almost entirely routine.
  • Insurance and pricing questions — the same five questions, dozens of times a week.
  • Hours, location, directions — 30-second calls that interrupt 10-minute tasks.
  • Confirmation callbacks — necessary, repetitive, and automatable.

Notice the pattern: the calls overwhelming your team are the easiest calls — routine, repeatable, and exactly what an AI receptionist for dental offices handles best. The hard calls (upset patients, complex cases) are rare — and those still go to your humans.
