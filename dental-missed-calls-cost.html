Where the calls go missing

Missed calls aren't one problem — they're two. The first happens during business hours: your front desk is checking in a patient, the phone rings four times, and the caller gives up. The second happens after hours: roughly 45% of patient calls arrive when the office is closed, and every one of them meets voicemail — if the patient even waits that long.

Practice owners consistently underestimate the number. The front desk remembers the calls they answered; nobody remembers the ones that rang out. Pull 30 days of your phone log and count the unanswered and abandoned calls. That's your real number.

Missed calls FAQs

How many calls do dental offices miss?

Industry call-tracking studies put the missed-call rate for dental practices at 32–38%. In one study of 4,280 calls across 26 practices, 38% went unanswered — mostly during business hours when the front desk was busy, plus all after-hours volume.

Do patients leave voicemails or call back?

Most don't. Only about 14% of new patients leave a voicemail, and the majority call the next practice on their list instead. A missed call is usually a lost patient, not a delayed one.

What is a missed new-patient call worth?

A single new patient represents the first visit plus years of cleanings, potential major work, and referrals — industry estimates put lifetime value at $3,000 or more. One missed implant or Invisalign inquiry can exceed $5,000.

When do most missed calls happen?

Two peaks: during business hours when the front desk is with in-person patients (calls ring out or hit hold), and after hours — roughly 45% of patient calls come outside office hours, when nobody is there to answer at all.

How do I find out how many calls my office misses?

Check your phone system's call log for unanswered and abandoned calls over 30 days, and compare against appointments booked from phone inquiries. Most practice owners are surprised — the number is almost always higher than the front desk estimates.
