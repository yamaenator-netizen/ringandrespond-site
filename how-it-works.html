How an after hours answering service for dentists works, step by step

No porting, no new hardware, no IT project. Sunny sits behind the phones you already have, answers the calls your front desk can't get to — in your practice's name — and hands your team one sorted callback list. Hear her before you read another word: (619) 202-1085.

The four steps, expanded

Step 1 — A short setup call

We set Sunny up the way your office actually runs: your hours, your services, how your office wants new and existing patients handled, and your urgent rules — what counts as a true emergency, what is urgent, and what can wait for the morning list. This is also where the paperwork order is fixed: the signed BAA comes before any patient information flows, as part of setup.

Step 2 — Keep your number

Your number does not move. Your phones, your carrier, and your practice system stay exactly where they are, and patients keep dialing the number they already know. If the office in the example is Your Dental Practice, callers still call Your Dental Practice's number — Sunny answers in that name, behind it.

Step 3 — Turn on forwarding

You switch on call forwarding for the calls your team can't take: overflow — when the line is busy or the desk is with a patient — and after hours, including the lunch gap if your office closes for it. From that moment, a call that would have rung out is answered by Sunny instead. The next section explains forwarding itself, in plain terms.

Step 4 — Change your mind anytime

There is no step four project. Turn forwarding off and your phone rings exactly like it used to — that is the whole cancellation process, and it works from the first day of the pilot. Plans are month-to-month, and the pilot's first month carries an unconditional money-back guarantee: email hello@ringandrespond.com within 30 days.

Go-live takes days, not weeks.

Forwarding is a setting on your existing phone line — nothing is replaced.

Call forwarding tells your phone line: when we can't pick up, send this call onward instead of letting it ring out. Your office turns it on for the two situations where calls are lost today — when the desk is already on a call or with a patient (overflow), and when the office is closed (after hours).

What the caller experiences

They dial your number, the phone is answered in your practice's name, and they have a real conversation — in English, Spanish, Arabic, or Farsi. Sunny says honestly that she is an AI assistant, discloses that the call is recorded, takes their name, reads their callback number back digit-by-digit, asks whether they are a new or existing patient and when they would prefer a callback, and captures the reason they called — including, for insurance questions, the carrier and their exact question, word for word.

What your team experiences

True emergencies are directed to 911 or urgent care with an instant alert to the team. Everything else lands on one callback list, urgencies first, ready before the office opens — plus a regular owner proof report of the week's calls, requests, and urgency mix.

What forwarding is not

It is not porting (your number never moves), it is not a new phone system, and it is not permanent. One setting, on or off, in your office's hands.

The dashboard: see everything in one place. IN BUILD

The office dashboard is where the whole picture gathers on one screen: today's calls, the callback list with urgencies first, the owner proof report, and Settings holding your office's own controls — the warm transfer toggle (ON: a true emergency can be connected live to the on-call dentist; OFF: Sunny captures the details, flags it urgent, and alerts the team instead), the on-call number, your languages, and your hours.

Coming with the dashboard: caller recognition and intent — every call labeled by what the caller wants (book, reschedule, billing, insurance, emergency), and repeat callers recognized from Sunny's own call history. With the Open Dental connection, a later phase adds full patient match on a caller card — name, next appointment, pending treatment. Neither phase is live today.

What Sunny does not do today. Stated before you ask, not after.

  • She does not book into your practice's schedule. She captures the request properly — name, callback number, new or existing patient, preferred callback window — and your team books when it calls back. Booking into Open Dental is coming, waiting on developer access; until it is live and proven, Sunny books nothing.
  • She never touches your patient database. Not to read it, not to update it. The information she works with is what the caller tells her on that call.
  • She does not verify insurance eligibility. She captures the carrier and the caller's exact question for your team to answer. Verification works in a test environment today; production comes with a signed pilot office.
  • She does not text callers back yet. Missed-call text-back is coming, waiting on carrier approval for business texting, with resubmission on November 13. The same wait applies to texted appointment confirmations, same-day backfill messages, and review requests by text — a QR-code version of a review request works today.
  • She does not work recall lists yet. Recall and reactivation runs on your office's own list are coming, still being built and proven.
  • She does not take patient payments. A patient billing portal, where patients pay the office directly, is a later phase.
  • Warm transfer is an option, not a default behavior. It is your office's ON/OFF toggle, and it stays a spec item until the transfer path is built and tested end-to-end.
